How the ranking works

For the selected chord, every note of a candidate scale is classified relative to the chord: chord tone (defines the harmony), tension (consonant extension — 9, 13, etc.), color tension (usable but characterful — ♯11 on a major chord, altered notes on a dominant), avoid note (dissonant against a chord tone, usable in passing), or clash (contradicts the chord quality).

The inside score blends two things: the average consonance of the scale's notes (tone 1.0, tension 0.75, color 0.55, avoid 0.3, clash 0) and how many of the chord's defining tones the scale actually contains. 100% ≈ pure chord-sound; low percentages are deliberately "outside" superimpositions that create tension you must resolve.

Scales that contain identical notes are merged into one row — over Cmaj7, C Ionian, D Dorian and G Mixolydian are the same eleven fingers' worth of pitches, so they appear once with their equivalent names listed.
